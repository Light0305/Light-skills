The tension between the narrator’s description of town T as silent and the later marketplace scene’s overlapping voices raises a question about what silence means within the narrative. Read together, the scenes invite an account of silence as selective recognition: speech may be present without counting as part of the town’s narrated identity. On this reading, the marketplace complicates the earlier description by exposing a distinction between the presence of voices and the narrator’s acknowledgment of them. The contrast could, however, register a change in time or setting rather than selective narration; the relationship between the scenes leaves that alternative open. The proposed reading therefore turns on whether their differences can be understood as more than situational variation. Its interpretive payoff is to make silence a problem of narrative recognition, directing attention to whose speech becomes perceptible within the account of town T.
