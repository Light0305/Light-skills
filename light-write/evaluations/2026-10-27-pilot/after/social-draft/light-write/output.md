The interviews with 18 purposively sampled nurses from two wards at one hospital revealed a tension between greater scheduling autonomy under the new shift-allocation policy and informal obligations to cover shifts. The autonomy theme suggested greater scope to shape schedules, while the counter-theme showed how pressure to provide cover could limit that scope. These themes were not confined to separate accounts: some participants described both, presenting autonomy as an opportunity qualified by obligation. Their coexistence suggests that formal discretion and the perceived freedom to exercise it were distinct aspects of scheduling. Informal pressure therefore complicates an interpretation of the policy as simply expanding choice. In these accounts, greater autonomy could coexist with expectations to cover shifts, making scheduling choice conditional rather than unrestricted.
