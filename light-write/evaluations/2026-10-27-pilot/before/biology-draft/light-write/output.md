The knockdown and rescue results support a functional contribution of A to maintaining marker B in cell line L: the reduction after A depletion was largely reversed by A re-expression. These findings, obtained from four independent culture preparations, extend the previously reported association between A and B by providing perturbation-based evidence in this cellular context. However, the small change in line M, with uncertainty encompassing no change, limits generalization across cell lines; without rescue data in M, the basis of this discrepancy remains unresolved. The evidence does not establish direct regulation, physical binding, or a specific pathway linking A to B. Indirect effects and differences in cellular context remain plausible explanations. A discriminating next experiment would compare matched degrees of A depletion followed by re-expression in both lines while measuring A and B, testing whether the discrepant responses persist when perturbation strength is comparable. Such persistence would support context dependence over unequal depletion as an explanation.
