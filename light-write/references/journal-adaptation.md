# Journal, conference, and style adaptation

Use when the user names a journal, conference, audience, or style target. Generic writing remains the default. Read `references/module-routing.md` for precedence and applicability; check `modules/catalog.json` before assuming an exact venue profile exists.

## Resolve the actual target

A useful target identifies an exact venue, article type, edition/year where relevant, and submission/revision/production stage. Publisher families and rankings are not styles. IEEE spans many journals/conferences; CCF categories describe ranking metadata; Science, Cell, and CVPR are distinct venues, not interchangeable evidence or formatting rules. Ambiguous acronyms need confirmation before venue-specific rules are applied. A named target does not justify changing study facts or forcing a discipline into another field's conventions.

When adopting `light-journal-select` output, read its actual `light-write-brief.md` and available selection packet/report, including source dates, article type, publication route, stage, input versions, and unresolved requirements. Its 1.1 audit separates `handoff.target_authorized` from `handoff.evidence_ready`: an author's chosen target can still have policy/evidence gaps, and an evidence-ready proposal is not an author decision. A provisional target permits generic argument/outline work; label venue-dependent choices as provisional instead of blocking all writing or silently treating a recommendation as authorization. Older 1.0 packets remain limited inputs, not automatic proof of current readiness.

## Adaptation sequence

1. Use applicable domain, genre, and section guidance first to understand what the paper must establish.
2. If a matching venue module exists, inspect its scope, maturity, provenance, and dates. An older rule is not automatically current; a reviewed style profile is not an official policy.
3. Verify hard constraints against a current official source before asserting exact word/page/display-item limits, section formats, data/code duties, or submission requirements. Record the source and actual access date. If verification is unavailable, disclose it and avoid presenting remembered limits as policy.
4. Label editorial patterns from examples, corpus studies, or experience as recommendations. Explain what a pattern helps accomplish without copying distinctive article wording or claiming a guarantee of acceptance.
5. Adapt framing, level of explanation, terminology, and length within evidence boundaries. Preserve contrary evidence and limitations. If a hard limit conflicts with reporting integrity, state the unresolved conflict rather than hide evidence.

## Style without imitation

When a user requests a named style, translate that into explicit editorial choices appropriate to the audience: how much context to supply, how to frame the question, which evidence to foreground, and how to state the contribution. Concision and accessible significance can be useful in broad-audience journals; a technical conference may need different methodological or evaluation detail. These are context-dependent choices, not universal venue laws or a fixed prose formula. Do not weaken a proof, oversimplify an interpretation, or inflate novelty to fit a brand image.

## When there is no profile

Continue using the general core and applicable field/genre modules. User-supplied author instructions can be used as supplied material, with verification status made clear. External verification occurs only through an available authorized research workflow; this skill has no bundled live rules engine. New profiles should follow `templates/venue-module.md` and `standards/modules.md`, including stale-rule handling and evaluation provenance.

## Reporting obligations and declarations

For a whole manuscript or submission package, identify applicable reporting guidance by actual study design, field, article type, and current venue rules. Health-research guidance can be found in the [EQUATOR library](https://equator-network.org/reporting-guidelines/index.html) (checked 2026-11-06). Use applicable items to map reported information and real gaps; a completed reporting checklist is neither a design-quality appraisal nor proof that a procedure occurred. Do not impose health-research checklists on unrelated disciplines.

Check only declarations relevant to the requested deliverable: ethics/consent, registration, funding/conflicts, author contributions, data/code availability, and AI assistance where applicable. Draft from actual records or author-confirmed facts; do not invent approvals, registrations, repository deposits, contributor roles, or a declaration of “no AI use.” Missing administrative facts can stay explicit gaps while supported prose proceeds.

For venues following ICMJE, its [Use of AI by Authors](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/artificial-intelligence/ai-use-by-authors.html) guidance (checked 2026-11-06) calls for disclosure of how AI was used, excludes AI tools from authorship, and keeps responsibility with human authors. Apply the target venue's actual instructions and the author's real use; this biomedical guidance is not a universal declaration template. Recheck affected requirements on retargeting or formal submission; ordinary local polishing does not require a new policy search.
