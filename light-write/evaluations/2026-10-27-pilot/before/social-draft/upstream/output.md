Accounts of the new shift-allocation policy revealed a tension between greater scheduling autonomy and informal obligations to cover shifts. In interviews with 18 purposively sampled nurses from two wards at one hospital, the author-supplied analysis identified greater autonomy as a theme, alongside a counter-theme of informal pressure that limited this autonomy. These themes were not mutually exclusive: some participants described both within the same account. Their coexistence suggests that perceived gains in choice could remain constrained by expectations to provide cover. Formal autonomy therefore did not necessarily translate into an equivalent sense of freedom when making scheduling decisions. The analytic distinction was between the autonomy associated with the policy and the informal obligations described alongside it, showing how increased choice and pressure to cover shifts could coexist in nurses’ experiences.
