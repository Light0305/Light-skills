The contrast between the narrator’s initial description of the village as empty and the later account of a crowded square raises a question about how inhabitants become visible within the narrative. Read together, the episodes suggest that emptiness may register the narrator’s selective recognition of the villagers rather than their physical absence. On this reading, the contrast directs attention to the limits of the narrator’s perspective; it does not establish the author’s intention to erase the inhabitants. A difference in the time of day, however, could also account for the change, and the excerpts leave that explanation open. The tension therefore supports an inquiry into narrative recognition, while leaving unresolved whether the contrast reflects selective perception or changing circumstances.
