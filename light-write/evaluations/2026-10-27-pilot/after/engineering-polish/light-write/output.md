The material exhibited directional thermal transport at 295 K, with thermal conductivities of 210 W m−1 K−1 parallel to the alignment axis and 24 W m−1 K−1 perpendicular to it, measured in three specimens per orientation. This contrast indicates anisotropic conductivity under the tested conditions. The aligned structure is a plausible explanation, although its contribution was not experimentally isolated. Unreported uncertainty intervals limit assessment of the precision of these values, and measurements confined to 295 K cannot establish suitability for high-temperature applications. A focused follow-up could compare specimens with controlled differences in alignment at 295 K to test whether conductivity varies with alignment.
