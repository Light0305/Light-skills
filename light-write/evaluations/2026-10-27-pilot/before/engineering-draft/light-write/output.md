This study examines how geometry and interface resistance affect predicted thermal-device output at 300 K and how the predictions compare with prototype performance. A coupled finite-element model predicted 12 W for geometry G with an ideal interface, compared with 10 W for reference geometry H under the same assumptions. Incorporating measured contact resistance reduced the prediction for G to 9 W, highlighting the sensitivity of its idealized performance to interface conditions. Because H was not re-evaluated with that resistance, the adjusted predictions do not establish a comparative advantage. A single prototype of G produced 8.7 W at 300 K; repeatability and performance at other temperatures remain uncharacterized. These results connect interface-sensitive modelling with a prototype measurement at one temperature, supporting explicit treatment of contact resistance when assessing the design.
