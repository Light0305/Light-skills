Use case: scientific-educational / infographic-diagram.
Create a completely new, dense, publication-style multi-scale mechanistic schematic of a rechargeable lithium–sulfur battery with a functional separator. This is a non-data scientific illustration for materials researchers, not an infographic poster. The intended message is that sulfur conversion, selective transport at the separator and anode-side parasitic reactions are coupled across scales. Use a very wide landscape canvas, aspect ratio 3:2, high resolution, four linked lettered panels. The figure should be substantially more elaborate than a simple cell cartoon: layered isometric geometry, many individually visible pores and particles, several species, two opposite transport processes, molecular conversion and interface comparison. Keep scientific relationships unambiguous and all labels readable.

VISUAL STRUCTURE
White paper background. Four thin light-grey rounded rectangular panel frames with generous clear gutters. Panel (a) dominates the upper-left, about 57% of the width and 55% of the height. Panel (b) occupies the upper-right, about 39% width and 55% height. Panel (c) is lower-left, about 49% width and 38% height. Panel (d) is lower-right, about 47% width and 38% height. Leave a short title inside each panel at its top edge. Draw two fine grey dashed callout leaders from the matching regions of panel (a) to the lower panels. They mean geometric enlargement, not electrochemical transport. All objects are clean editable-vector-looking forms, shallow isometric layers, restrained gradients. No realistic photography or raster microscopy. Use dark charcoal sans-serif labels with proper chemical subscripts; short leaders touch the actual entity.

PANEL (a), CELL ARCHITECTURE AND TRANSPORT
Draw a large exploded oblique cutaway of a layered cell, suspended within this panel. Its longitudinal left-to-right order is lithium-metal anode, liquid electrolyte space, functional separator, liquid-filled porous sulfur/carbon cathode, and a narrow cathode current collector. Give the foils and separator clear front faces, top faces and side faces, not flat rectangles. Lithium foil is cool silver-blue. Electrolyte is an extremely pale transparent blue enclosure. The separator is a beige fibrous mesh sheet with a thin teal functional layer on its cathode-facing right surface. The sulfur/carbon cathode is a dark charcoal interconnected porous skeleton containing numerous amber sulfur particles, with visible electrolyte-filled pores. The current collector is a narrow muted grey metallic sheet. Do not draw a solid conductive path through separator pores.
Above the cell draw a thin external wire with a compact load symbol, connecting the Li anode terminal to the cathode collector terminal. Its orange arrow points from the anode on the left to the cathode on the right, labelled "e- during discharge". The wire does not enter the electrolyte. Inside the cell draw several distinct blue Li+ arrows from the lithium anode through open separator channels towards the cathode, with a few blue ion circles. Draw purple dashed arrows indicating unwanted soluble polysulfide migration in the opposite direction, from cathode towards anode. Most purple chains are retained on the cathode-facing teal coating; a faint purple leakage route illustrates the undesired shuttle. Do not show absolute complete blocking. Use several small amber bead chains in solution as schematic polysulfides, not an atomistically resolved structure. Place concise labels outside the layer stack with leaders.

PANEL (b), SULFUR CONVERSION
Use a compact but richly illustrated vertical reaction network, four separated stages from top to bottom. Top stage: a schematic eight-bead amber S8 ring, labelled "S8 (solid)". Second stage: a pale lavender band with three different short bead-chain icons and exactly three labels "Li2S8", "Li2S6", "Li2S4", enclosed under the group label "Soluble polysulfides". Third stage: shorter, densely associated beads labelled "Li2S2 (solid)". Bottom stage: a compact aggregate of amber sulfur and small blue Li circles labelled "Li2S (solid)". These are conceptual species icons, not precise molecular conformations. A sequence of black downward arrows connects the four stages, with "Discharge" beside the downward path. A distinct purple upward return route to the right is labelled "Charge". Do not add stoichiometric coefficients to the individual intermediate arrows. At the bottom of this panel set the single overall net equation exactly "S8 + 16Li ⇌ 8Li2S". Keep this equation separated from the stages and fully inside the panel. No voltage axis, potential profile, reaction energy diagram, invented activation barrier or numerical capacity.

PANEL (c), FUNCTIONAL SEPARATOR ZOOM
Draw a large oblique teal/beige porous membrane, with a clear network of narrow channels and pore openings. Show three spatially separated mechanisms in the same zoom, from left to right, with distinct short leaders: a tortuous pore trapping a short amber chain, labelled "Physical confinement"; polar teal surface sites holding amber chains via thin dashed attraction lines, labelled "Polar adsorption"; a few teal catalytic sites close to short sulfur clusters with small curved conversion arrows, labelled "Catalytic conversion". Blue Li+ spheres pass through the open channels with blue arrows; longer amber chain symbols are mostly retained on the cathode-facing side. These are generic schematic functional sites, without an unverified chemical formula or precise COF atomic lattice. Label the blue route "Li+ transport". Avoid a decorative honeycomb that implies a specific crystallographic structure; use clearly schematic irregular pores and scaffolding.

PANEL (d), ANODE INTERFACE COMPARISON
Two carefully aligned miniature cross-sections side by side within this panel. Left mini-view labelled "Shuttle-dominated interface": silver-blue lithium metal at the bottom, irregular coral-grey surface deposits, uneven blue Li+ trajectories, and incoming purple dashed polysulfide paths with small amber chains. Include one leader "Parasitic reactions" to the deposits. Right mini-view labelled "Controlled transport": the same geometric lithium substrate, a thin continuous pale teal interphase, evenly spaced blue Li+ trajectories and mostly retained polysulfide chains away from the metal surface. These views are a qualitative design comparison, not experimental before/after micrographs and not a claim that any specified separator alone guarantees a perfect Li interface. Under the two mini-views draw a compact connected row of three small neutral boxes: "Shuttle", "Li loss", "Passivation", with arrows in that order. Dashed teal intervention connectors from the membrane zoom terminate at the relevant transport paths; do not connect arbitrary chemical species to unrelated processes.

PALETTE AND MARKS
Sulfur species amber/gold, Li+ small blue circles, carbon dark charcoal, separator beige with teal functional layer, Li metal silver-blue, unwanted polysulfide migration purple dashed arrows, parasitic deposits muted coral. Use shapes, labels and line styles in addition to colour. Uniform thin charcoal contours, clear medium arrowheads; no shadows that obscure layer boundaries. Use translucent fill only in electrolyte; light pastel gradients are allowed on solid layer faces. Keep individual particles and channels visible, without overcrowding text.
