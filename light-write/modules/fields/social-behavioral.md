# Social and behavioral research

- **ID:** social-behavioral
- **Maturity:** draft
- **Scope:** Quantitative, qualitative, and mixed-methods writing about people, institutions, behavior, and social settings.
- **Out of scope:** A universal social-science epistemology, automatic reporting-checklist compliance, or venue-specific rules.
- **Provenance:** Editorial starting guide; no field-expert review or corpus evaluation.
- **Last reviewed:** Not reviewed

## Argument and evidence load

Identify the actual inferential approach: estimation, causal identification, interpretation, ethnography, discourse analysis, theory development, or a combination. A thematic interpretation and a population estimate have different evidence loads. Neither should be judged by a statistical template merely because both concern people.

## Writing decisions

- **Introduction:** Define constructs and the theoretical/social problem. Link a gap to supplied literature without treating a different context as automatically novel or assuming a measure captures the whole construct.
- **Quantitative methods/results:** Preserve sampling, operationalization, design, identification assumptions, estimation, uncertainty, and exploratory status. Distinguish association, modeled adjustment, and causal identification. Do not invent representativeness or population coverage from sample size.
- **Qualitative methods/findings:** Preserve the documented analytic approach, positionality/context where relevant, and the relationship between interpretation and actual evidence. Quote only supplied, authorized excerpts with appropriate attribution/anonymization; do not invent participant voices, saturation, coding agreement, or member checks.
- **Mixed methods:** State how evidence strands relate rather than treating agreement as proof or concealing tensions. Do not collapse incompatible units or scopes of inference.
- **Discussion:** Connect findings to theory with explicit limits of setting, participants, measurement, and interpretation. Theme frequency does not automatically establish prevalence; lack of a theme does not prove absence.
- **Language:** Keep construct names distinct from instruments/proxies. Avoid stereotyping groups and preserve the author's scientifically meaningful contextual and reflexive qualifications.

## Common failure modes

| Failure | Corrective check |
|---|---|
| Interview themes become population prevalence | What sampling and analytic method support that inference? |
| Covariate adjustment is labeled causal identification | What identification assumptions and design are supplied? |
| A proxy is treated as the construct itself | How was the construct operationalized and bounded? |
| Narrative coherence is achieved by fabricated quotations | Is each quote present and authorized in the input? |

## Interactions

Use clinical-health for health contexts without imposing trial conventions on qualitative studies; humanities may help interpretive arguments; computing may help computational components. Do not let statistical or narrative conventions override the author's documented methodological approach.
