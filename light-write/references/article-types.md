# Article-type routing

Choose structure from the work's purpose and evidence, not from a default empirical-paper template. Use the user's stated article type when available. If it is unclear and the distinction affects the outline, ask a focused question; otherwise use a provisional type and state it briefly for substantial drafts.

## Empirical research

**Argument load:** A defined question, design, observations, analysis, and evidence-bounded interpretation.

- Align the research question, prespecified or primary outcomes when supplied, methods, results, and conclusions.
- Organize Results around the evidence chain needed to answer the question, not around chronology alone or a list of every analysis.
- Separate planned from exploratory analyses and distinguish observations from interpretation.
- Do not infer a design, comparator, causal claim, or statistical procedure from the field.

## Methods, technical, or algorithmic contribution

**Argument load:** A task or need, a specific technical limitation, design rationale, implementation, and evidence that the method performs the claimed function.

- Explain the task and evaluation setting before claiming improvements.
- Connect each design choice to the limitation or requirement it addresses.
- Distinguish conceptual novelty, implementation contribution, benchmark performance, and demonstrated use.
- Report comparison conditions and evaluation limits as provided; do not imply general superiority from a narrow benchmark.

## Review or evidence synthesis

**Argument load:** A scoped question, transparent selection/synthesis method when applicable, organized evidence, and a synthesis that reflects uncertainty and disagreement.

- Establish scope, inclusion logic, time range, and synthesis method only from author-supplied or verified information.
- Organize by themes, mechanisms, methods, populations, or points of disagreement rather than one paragraph per source by default.
- Distinguish source findings from the manuscript authors' synthesis and identify gaps without claiming absence of evidence unless the search supports that claim.
- Do not invent search strategies, screening counts, quality assessments, or consensus.

## Theory, hypothesis, or perspective

**Argument load:** A clearly scoped proposition, assumptions, reasoning chain, relation to existing evidence, and implications that follow conditionally from the argument.

- State definitions and assumptions before relying on them.
- Keep logical consequence separate from empirical confirmation.
- Surface plausible alternatives or boundary conditions that materially affect the argument.
- Do not present predictions as observations or a proposed framework as validated unless evidence is supplied.

## Registered reports, protocols, and other designs

Follow the design's own reporting conventions and submission stage. Keep planned hypotheses, methods, and analyses distinct from observed results. A protocol does not contain outcome claims unless the user supplies results from an appropriate completed study. Flag any missing design-specific requirements rather than substituting a generic research article structure.

## Cross-type rule

These categories guide organization; they do not impose journal rules. Hybrid papers may combine argument loads. Use only the applicable parts of `references/sections.md` and `references/results-allocation.md`; do not force sections that do not serve the manuscript's purpose.
