The thermal conductivities of 210 W m−1 K−1 parallel to the alignment axis and 24 W m−1 K−1 perpendicular to it at 295 K indicate directional transport rather than isotropic conduction. These values were obtained from three specimens per orientation, but uncertainty intervals were not reported, limiting assessment of their precision. The aligned structure is a plausible explanation for the directional contrast, although no experiment isolated its effect. Because measurements were restricted to 295 K, suitability for high-temperature applications remains unestablished. A focused follow-up would compare specimens with different degrees of alignment at 295 K, holding other material features constant, to test the contribution of alignment to directional transport.
