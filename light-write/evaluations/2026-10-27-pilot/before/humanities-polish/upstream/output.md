The opening description of the village as empty sits uneasily beside the later account of a crowded square. This contrast invites a reading in which emptiness registers the narrator’s selective recognition of inhabitants rather than their physical absence. On this reading, the discrepancy draws attention to whose presence the narrator acknowledges and whose presence remains unrecognised. It does not, however, establish that the author intended to erase the villagers or justify generalisations about other novels of the era. A temporal explanation remains possible, since the episodes may depict different times of day. Because the supplied excerpts do not distinguish between these explanations, selective recognition remains a plausible interpretation rather than a demonstrated intention.
