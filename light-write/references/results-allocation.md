# Results allocation and compression audit

Use for empirical Results sections, substantial main-text shortening, or a request to decide which supplied findings belong in the narrative versus captions, Methods, Source Data, or supplementary material. This is an editorial organization task; it does not analyze or reanalyze data.

## 1. Inventory findings before editing

Record each supplied finding and its role. A finding may serve more than one role.

| Category | Typical role | Main-text question |
|---|---|---|
| Primary finding | Directly answers the research question | Is it indispensable to the central evidence chain? |
| Necessary support | Validates a measure, comparison, or interpretation | Would omitting it make the main finding hard to assess? |
| Qualification or contrary result | Limits, weakens, or changes a conclusion | Would the reader reach a materially different interpretation without it? |
| Robustness or sensitivity | Tests dependence on assumptions or analytic choices | Does it materially alter confidence, direction, or scope? |
| Secondary or exploratory analysis | Extends beyond the primary question | Is it central to the stated contribution, or can details be moved while retaining a signpost? |
| Heterogeneity or subgroup | Shows for whom/under what conditions a result holds | Does it change generality or expose a material boundary? |
| Provenance/reproducibility detail | Explains data, materials, transformations, or pipeline | Is concise context needed here, with full detail elsewhere? |
| Edge case or implementation detail | Documents a boundary or a non-central technical step | Does it affect interpretation, reproducibility, safety, or validity? |

Do not label an analysis exploratory, secondary, or robustness unless the supplied source establishes that status; otherwise mark it unknown and ask only if the distinction matters.

## 2. Apply the interpretation-change test

For each result, ask whether omitting or moving it would change the reader's understanding of:

- the direction, magnitude, precision, or reliability of the primary finding;
- the population, conditions, comparator, or outcome to which the claim applies;
- a plausible alternative explanation or the claim's causal/mechanistic strength;
- whether the result is primary, exploratory, null, contradictory, or qualified;
- the ethical, safety, reproducibility, or reporting interpretation.

If yes, keep the relevant evidence visible in the main narrative or provide an explicit in-text qualification with an accessible, precise pointer to supporting details. A cleaner story is not a reason to hide a result that changes the conclusion.

## 3. Choose a destination

Use the manuscript's field and journal conventions, and do not assume every journal accepts the same materials or structures.

| Destination | Put material here when… | Minimum main-text connection |
|---|---|---|
| Results prose | The finding is part of the shortest adequate evidence chain or changes interpretation | State the finding, comparison, and essential qualification; refer to its display item if supplied |
| Figure/table caption | Detail is needed to interpret a displayed result, groups, symbols, or statistical annotation | State the displayed result's role in the text; do not duplicate the entire results narrative |
| Methods | Procedures, definitions, model details, or analysis choices explain how evidence was produced | Point readers to the method needed to interpret a result; report deviations where required |
| Source Data/repository | Underlying values, materials, or full analysis artefacts are meant to be accessible there | Identify the relevant resource accurately; never claim a file exists unless supplied |
| Supplementary material | Secondary detail, full sensitivity analysis, additional examples, or technical derivations are useful but not central | Give a precise in-text pointer and summarize any outcome that changes interpretation |

Reporting standards, ethics, safety, and reproducibility requirements take precedence over an editorial preference for shorter text. Do not claim that material can be moved to a particular destination without checking that the user has it and the target journal permits the format.

## 4. Build the minimum adequate chain

Order the main Results narrative by the logic required to answer the question, not merely by when analyses were performed. For each key claim, preserve enough information for a reader to understand what was compared, the outcome, and the uncertainty or qualification needed to interpret it. Keep descriptive and inferential statistics where needed by the field and reporting standard; do not decide that a statistic is “secondary” without evidence or author direction.

Assign each repeated claim a distinct role (for example, question/setup, evidence, synthesis, or implication). Remove a repetition only if doing so loses no evidence, qualification, or useful orientation. Avoid repeating full statistical details in prose and captions when one clear presentation plus a useful cross-reference suffices, subject to journal and reporting rules.

## 5. Audit a compression or restructuring

For substantial edits, provide a concise audit when useful:

| Finding/claim | Before location | After location | Change | Rationale / author check |
|---|---|---|---|---|

For a substantial audit, also record the location of statistics supporting each major claim: the descriptive measure, denominator, inferential result and uncertainty where supplied, and the precise location of any relocated detail. Track repeated claims by section and role (setup, evidence, qualification, synthesis, implication); flag duplication that serves no distinct purpose. These records may be combined with the allocation table rather than emitted as separate tables.

Record before/after word counts only when counts can be measured from the supplied text and the user needs them. Specify the counting scope (for example, Results prose only, excluding captions). If a requested limit conflicts with evidence integrity, preserve the necessary material and report the unresolved excess rather than silently deleting it.

Do not provide a complete allocation table for a local edit unless requested. Preserve all analysis details in the paper's appropriate locations; this guide does not authorize deleting data, changing analysis, or inventing missing displays.
