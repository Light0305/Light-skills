The contrast between the narrator’s initial description of the village as empty and the later account of a crowded square raises a question about what the narrator recognizes as presence. Read together, these episodes suggest that the initial description may register a selective recognition of inhabitants rather than their physical absence. On this reading, the discrepancy draws attention to the limits of the narrator’s perspective; it does not establish the author’s intention to erase the villagers. A temporal explanation remains equally unresolved: the episodes may depict different times of day. Because the supplied excerpts do not distinguish between these possibilities, selective recognition remains a provisional interpretation, offering no basis for generalizing about the ideology of other novels of the era.
