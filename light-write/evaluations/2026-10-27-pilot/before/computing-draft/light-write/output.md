When images contain both object features and background cues, performance on an unchanged split leaves open how much a model depends on the background. The baseline considered here, B, learns from full images, leaving both sources of information available. We investigate whether restricting feature aggregation to foreground regions during training reduces background dependence. A cross-background test that changes backgrounds while preserving object identity provides a targeted evaluation of this question, alongside the original split.

We introduce method F, which uses supplied foreground masks during training but requires no masks at inference. F and B share a backbone and train/test split. In this synthetic benchmark, F achieves lower accuracy on the original split than B (81.2% versus 83.0%) but higher accuracy on the cross-background split (74.5% versus 62.8%). This trade-off supports foreground-restricted training as an approach to reducing background dependence under the tested conditions, while showing a cost in original-split accuracy. With one training run per method and no external-dataset evaluation or annotation-cost analysis, the evidence remains limited to this benchmark comparison.
