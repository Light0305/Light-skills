At 295 K, thermal conductivity was 210 W m−1 K−1 parallel to the alignment axis and 24 W m−1 K−1 perpendicular to it. These measurements, obtained from three specimens per orientation, indicate directional rather than isotropic transport. The aligned structure offers a plausible explanation, although its contribution was not isolated experimentally. Uncertainty intervals were not reported, limiting assessment of the precision of these conductivity estimates. Measurements were confined to 295 K, leaving suitability for high-temperature applications untested. A focused follow-up could compare specimens with controlled differences in alignment at 295 K to test whether alignment contributes to directional transport.
