The design question is whether the predicted output advantage of thermal-device geometry G over reference geometry H persists when contact resistance is included. At 300 K, a coupled finite-element model predicts 12 W for G with an ideal interface, compared with 10 W for H under the same assumptions. Introducing measured contact resistance lowers the prediction for G to 9 W, showing that interface conditions materially affect its predicted performance. H was not re-evaluated with this resistance, leaving the comparison under resistive interface conditions unresolved. A single prototype of G produced 8.7 W at 300 K, close to the resistance-inclusive prediction, although repeatability was not assessed. Together, the model sensitivity and prototype measurement characterize G at 300 K and identify contact resistance as a consequential design consideration, while leaving its comparative performance under matched interface conditions unresolved.
