# Synthetic worked examples

All studies, values, terms, and excerpts below are **synthetic teaching examples**. They do not describe real research and include no real citations. Their purpose is to demonstrate the writing decisions, not a preferred scientific conclusion.

## Example 1: from evidence ledger to Discussion paragraph

### Supplied synthetic facts

- A hypothetical two-site observational cohort includes 240 adults with condition Q.
- In the adjusted analysis, higher marker M is associated with lower symptom score S at week 8: beta = -0.30 score units per 1-unit increase in M; 95% CI, -0.48 to -0.12.
- The study is observational; unmeasured confounding is possible.
- The user supplies no previous-study citations or mechanism experiments.

### Compact claim–evidence record

| Proposed claim | Support | Status and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Higher M is associated with lower S at week 8 in the studied cohort | Adjusted estimate and interval | Supported by supplied analysis; observational association, two-site cohort only |
| M reduces symptoms | No intervention or causal identification supplied | Not supported; do not state as a causal effect |
| M may act through pathway P | No mechanism experiment supplied | Missing evidence; omit or mark as a hypothesis only if requested |

### Paragraph plan

1. State the principal association with its population and time boundary.
2. Interpret the association without causal language.
3. Name the confounding limitation and the resulting boundary on inference.
4. Identify a future test only if the author wants implications; do not invent a specific experiment as if it were performed.

### Example draft

In this two-site cohort of adults with condition Q, higher marker M was associated with lower symptom scores at week 8. The adjusted estimate was -0.30 score units per 1-unit increase in M (95% CI, -0.48 to -0.12). Because the analysis was observational, residual confounding cannot be excluded; the result therefore supports an association in the studied cohort but does not establish that M reduces symptoms.

### Calibration note

“was associated with” reflects the supplied observational design. “M reduced symptoms” would exceed the evidence. The example does not add a citation, infer a mechanism, or generalize to other populations.

## Example 2: allocation and qualification

### Supplied synthetic findings

A hypothetical intervention study reports a positive primary outcome at the prespecified endpoint, a null result on a secondary exploratory outcome, and a sensitivity analysis that weakens the primary estimate but does not reverse its direction. No counts or numerical estimates are supplied.

### Editorial allocation

| Finding | Provisional role | Placement decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Positive prespecified primary outcome | Primary | Main Results | Directly answers the central question; exact estimate still needed |
| Null exploratory outcome | Secondary, explicitly exploratory per supplied description | Briefly report in Results; detailed display may be supplementary if available | Prevents presenting the intervention as uniformly beneficial |
| Sensitivity analysis weakens but does not reverse estimate | Qualification/robustness | Keep the weakening visible in the main narrative; place full details in an available supplement and point to it | It changes confidence in magnitude even if direction is stable |

A suitable text would need a specific placeholder before it could be manuscript-ready: `[Evidence needed: provide the primary outcome estimate, uncertainty interval, and sensitivity-analysis estimate]`. Do not create numerical values or claim that a supplementary file exists.

## Example 3: terminology collision

A draft uses “response” to mean both a survey answer and a biological treatment response. Do not globally normalize these terms. Record the two meanings separately, propose disambiguating labels, and ask the author to confirm the preferred names if the distinction affects interpretation. After confirmation, update all occurrences and rerun the terminology check.
