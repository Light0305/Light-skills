On benchmark D, our method achieved 42.7 mean average precision (mAP), compared with 42.1 mAP for the baseline, a difference of 0.6 mAP. Both models were evaluated once on the same test split and used the same backbone. However, the baseline received half as many training updates as our method, limiting attribution of the observed gain to the method itself. Inference latency on one GPU at batch size 1 was 18 ms for our method versus 12 ms for the baseline. The higher observed mAP therefore came with 50% greater inference latency. Removing module R yielded 42.3 mAP in one run, but without repeated-seed results, these comparisons establish neither a reliable performance advantage nor the proposed mechanism.
