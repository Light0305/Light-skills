When object features and background cues co-occur in training images, performance on the original split alone cannot establish how well a model preserves object recognition under background changes. The full-image baseline B can aggregate information from both regions, leaving their contributions to prediction unresolved. This motivates a specific question: can restricting feature aggregation to foreground regions during training reduce background dependence, and what performance trade-off does this restriction introduce? A supplied cross-background test, which changes backgrounds while preserving object identity, provides a controlled setting for examining this question.

Here, we investigate method F, which uses supplied foreground masks during training but requires no masks at inference. F and B share a backbone and train/test split. In the available runs, F improves cross-background accuracy from 62.8% to 74.5%, while original-split accuracy falls from 83.0% to 81.2%. This trade-off motivates foreground-restricted training as a candidate approach to background robustness within this synthetic benchmark. With one training run per method, the comparison leaves run-to-run reliability unresolved; external-dataset transfer and mask annotation costs also remain unevaluated.
