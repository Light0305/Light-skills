Knockdown and re-expression in cell line L support a functional contribution of A to maintaining marker B: the decrease after knockdown was largely reversed when A was restored. Evidence from four independent culture preparations strengthens this interpretation within L, although rescue does not identify the intervening molecular steps. The small, uncertain change in cell line M limits generalization and raises the possibility that dependence of B on A varies with cellular context. These findings extend the previously reported association between A and B by providing perturbation-and-rescue evidence in L, while leaving direct regulation and pathway identity unresolved; no direct-binding experiment was conducted. A discriminating next test would compare knockdown and rescue in both lines while verifying comparable A depletion and restoration. A persistent difference would support context-dependent responsiveness, whereas convergence under matched perturbation would favor differences in perturbation effectiveness as an explanation. Either outcome would refine the functional relationship without, by itself, establishing a molecular mechanism.
