Thermal-device geometry must be assessed together with interface losses to determine whether idealized performance gains remain plausible in a prototype. We examine this design question for geometry G using a coupled finite-element model and a prototype measurement at 300 K. With an ideal interface, the model predicts 12 W for G, compared with 10 W for reference geometry H under the same assumptions. Incorporating measured contact resistance lowers the prediction for G to 9 W, showing the sensitivity of its predicted output to interface losses. A single prototype of G produces 8.7 W at 300 K. Because H was not re-evaluated with contact resistance, these results do not establish an advantage under matched interface conditions. The study provides a model-to-prototype assessment of G at 300 K, with repeatability and performance at other temperatures unresolved.
